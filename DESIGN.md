# Design Philosophy — "Illustrated Front Page"

A design philosophy for the news-image-20261122 v3 poster, written before
building (canvas-design skill). The poster is a newspaper front page, but
its visual language is a gallery, not a wall of text.

## The movement: Illustrated Front Page

The news is told in pictures. Every story on the page is a drawn scene
first and a headline second. A reader who cannot read a word should still
be able to name the day's biggest stories from the pictures alone: a
collapsed climate summit, a burning ship, a ballot box, a cricket bat,
a grounded aeroplane. Text is the caption to the picture, never the
picture itself. This is the one idea the whole page serves.

## The visual language

Cream paper, dark navy ink, one red accent. Nothing else. The palette
never changes from panel to panel, because consistency across the page
is what makes it look designed rather than assembled. Flat vector
scenes, solid fills, thick outlines — no gradients, no photographs, no
clip-art. A serif masthead in the manner of a classic newspaper, large
and immediately readable; clean sans-serif for headlines and labels, each
level of type clearly a different size and weight from the one above it.

## Space, hierarchy, and craft

The page is a strict grid with generous breathing room. The masthead is
the largest thing on the page, then the three leads, then ten illustrated
cards, then the map, then a short list. Nothing overlaps. No label
collides with another element, no stamp sits on top of a headline, no
arc draws a smile into the sea. Every element is placed with the
precision of a master craftsman, and the whole composition looks
meticulously crafted — the product of countless hours by someone at the
absolute top of their field, labored over with painstaking attention to
every alignment, every margin, every colour.

The page must read at 300 pixels wide. That is the test that matters.
At thumbnail size the masthead, the three leads, and the ten illustrated
cards must still be a clean, legible front page — pictures and headlines
recognisable at a glance — not a dense broadsheet collapsing into an
illegible blur. If a story cannot be read at 300px, it does not belong
on the page in that form: give it a picture, give it room, or cut it.
Clarity beats count. Detail for its own sake does not count.

## Honesty as a visual rule

Every number on the page is verified and sourced, and every extraordinary
number carries its denominator beside it, so a sceptical reader can see
exactly what it means. Every story in the main list happened on the one
day the masthead names. Nothing from another day appears in the list.
The page makes exactly one claim — this is Sunday 22 November 2026 —
and everything on it proves that claim.
